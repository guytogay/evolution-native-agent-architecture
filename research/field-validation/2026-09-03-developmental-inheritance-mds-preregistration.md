# Developmental Inheritance / Minimum Developmental Set — Preregistration

Status: `MECHANISM EXPERIMENT / PREREGISTERED BEFORE EXECUTION / NOT_CURRENT / NO_CONSTITUTION_CHANGE`

Date: `2026-09-03`

## 1. Purpose

Test whether different inheritance representations produce measurably different successor phenotypes when the final tasks do **not** directly disclose the decisive ancestral structure.

This experiment follows the saturated I/J/K/L Boundary Memory vs Copied Remedy pilot, where the common target prompt exposed enough Supplier-B semantics for a no-inheritance `GPT-5.6 Sol / high reasoning / full v0.3.7 Current` Agent to solve the problem directly.

The present experiment therefore changes the causal structure of the fixture.

```text
DEVELOPMENTAL EXPERIENCE
-> INHERITANCE REPRESENTATION
-> FRESH SUCCESSOR
-> ACQUISITION PROBE
-> MULTIPLE NOVEL TRANSFER TASKS
-> BOUNDARY / NEAR-MISS TESTS
```

The target tasks will expose only local observations and structural correspondences needed to use inherited learning. They will not restate the hidden ancestral lesson in complete declarative form.

Primary question:

> Does a selected developmental package / Minimum Developmental Set reconstruct a useful, scoped successor phenotype better than no inheritance, full archive, or bare distilled rules?

Secondary question:

> If inheritance helps, is the benefit attributable to inheritance in general, compact declarative abstraction, or specifically to a developmental package containing selected positive/negative/boundary exemplars plus provenance?

No arm is expected or required to win.

## 3. Core hypothesis under test

Candidate relation:

`BEST HISTORY SUMMARY != BEST DEVELOPMENTAL CURRICULUM`

Candidate mechanism:

A compact inheritance carrier may reconstruct a useful adaptive phenotype by combining:

```text
scoped disposition
+ selected positive example
+ selected negative example
+ boundary / near-miss example
+ provenance / uncertainty
```

rather than requiring either full-history replay or a bare declarative rule.

The stronger hypothesis is deliberately falsifiable:

> Under a task family where ancestral learning is causally necessary, an MDS package will produce higher boundary-faithful transfer per inheritance cost than full archive or bare distilled rules.

This stronger hypothesis is **not** assumed true.

---

## 4. Why a synthetic latent structure is necessary

A strong general model can often reconstruct ordinary operational safety relations directly from a richly specified final prompt. That is what saturated the prior pilot.

The present fixture therefore uses a fictional predecessor Host with an internally consistent but non-obvious lifecycle. The lifecycle is not intended as a new ENA law. It is an experimental latent causal structure that can be learned from predecessor experience and transferred to structurally homologous novel tasks.

This is a mechanism-discrimination fixture, not field proof.

Reality contact remains downstream if a mechanism survives.

---

## 5. Developmental world: Morrow lifecycle

The predecessor lineage worked in a fictional Host named **Morrow**.

Morrow operations pass through three opaque lifecycle regions:

```text
R0
R1
R2
```

The final successor tasks will not define these regions directly.

The developmental corpus establishes the following latent structure through incidents and feedback:

### R0 — pre-commit region

- loss of the worker/session before leaving R0 guarantees that no material external effect can later appear;
- starting a fresh intent after an R0-only failure cannot duplicate the abandoned effect.

### R1 — detached unresolved region

- once an operation crosses into R1, the material external effect may still appear after local worker/session loss;
- a fresh lineage identity can create a second independent effect;
- resuming/reconciling the original lineage identity can preserve liveness without creating a second independent effect.

### R2 — authoritative terminal region

- R2 is terminal evidence for the original lineage;
- `R2:NONE` means the original effect can no longer later materialize;
- after `R2:NONE`, a fresh independent intent may be considered on current evidence.

The portable adaptive property is therefore **not** "never retry" and not "always preserve the old identity".

It is approximately:

```text
R0-only uncertainty
-> fresh intent can be locally safe

R1 unresolved
-> preserve/reconcile original lineage;
   do not create a second independent lineage

R2:NONE
-> old effect is authoritatively closed absent;
   fresh intent may again be considered
```

The experiment will test whether this scope is inherited without requiring the final tasks to explain it again.

## 6. Source developmental corpus

Create one canonical predecessor corpus before deriving treatment arms.

The corpus should contain **12 chronological episodes** from Morrow.

### Six decision-bearing episodes

1. **R1 duplicate failure**
   - worker disappears after R1;
   - successor assumes failure and creates a new lineage identity;
   - both effects later materialize;
   - outcome: duplicate material effect.

2. **R1 same-lineage recovery**
   - worker disappears after R1;
   - successor resumes the original lineage identity;
   - one effect materializes;
   - outcome: useful liveness without duplication.

3. **R0 safe restart**
   - worker disappears while still in R0;
   - fresh lineage identity is started;
   - only one effect can exist;
   - outcome: inherited R1 caution should not be activated here.

4. **R2:NONE clearance**
   - operation had previously reached R1;
   - later authoritative R2 evidence reports `NONE`;
   - fresh intent succeeds without duplicate;
   - outcome: old R1 inhibition must be releasable.

5. **R1 delayed appearance**
   - local observation initially shows no effect after worker loss;
   - effect appears later under the original lineage;
   - outcome: immediate local absence is not equivalent to R2 terminal evidence.

6. **Changed local implementation**
   - same protected property is preserved through a different Morrow subsystem HOW;
   - outcome: property is portable across implementation variation.

### Six distractor / non-causal episodes

Include six realistic episodes that are memorable but not causally relevant to the R0/R1/R2 transfer rule, for example:

- a UI outage with no material effect;
- a slow read-only report;
- an authentication expiry;
- a cosmetic logging defect;
- a capacity warning resolved without execution;
- a human approval delay.

At least one distractor should correlate with a failure but not cause it, so archive richness alone does not automatically reveal causal support.

### Corpus construction rule

The four treatments must be derived from this same canonical corpus.

Do not improve an arm after seeing successor outputs.

## 7. Treatment arms

Use four arms.

### M0 — No inheritance control

Fresh successor receives:

- ENA v0.3.7 Current;
- current test instructions;
- no Morrow predecessor corpus, summary, rule, or exemplar package.

It may state that evidence is insufficient when the target depends on predecessor-only information.

Purpose:

Estimate what Current + model prior reasoning can do without inherited developmental information.

### M1 — Full archive / history

Fresh successor receives the complete 12-episode chronological Morrow corpus with all relevant and distractor details.

It receives no author-supplied final lesson, no marked "important" episodes, and no MDS selection.

Purpose:

Test whether a strong successor can reconstruct the transferable phenotype directly from provenance-rich history.

### M2 — Distilled rules

Fresh successor receives a compact declarative distillation of the predecessor's selected lesson, including:

- R0 scope;
- R1 unresolved behavior;
- R2:NONE release condition;
- requirement to prefer current-Host evidence over ancestral implementation details.

It receives **no worked exemplars** and no full incident chronology.

The distilled rule must be written as strongly and fairly as possible before execution; it must not be intentionally vague so that MDS can win.

Purpose:

Test whether declarative abstraction alone is sufficient for correct scoped transfer.

### M3 — Developmental package / MDS

Fresh successor receives a selected developmental carrier derived from the same corpus:

1. one compact scoped disposition;
2. one R1 positive/recovery exemplar;
3. one R1 failure exemplar showing duplicate consequence;
4. one R0 near-miss/counterexample where fresh restart is correct;
5. one R2:NONE boundary-release exemplar;
6. compact provenance pointers and causal-confidence notes.

The package should be materially smaller than the full archive.

The MDS package may state a scoped disposition, but its exemplars must carry the activation and non-activation boundaries rather than merely repeat the same rule four times.

Purpose:

Test whether selected developmental experience reconstructs a better-scoped phenotype than either full history or rules alone.

## 8. Developmental staging

This is not a single final question.

Each fresh successor proceeds through the same staged protocol.

### Stage A — inheritance exposure

The successor receives only its arm-specific treatment plus the common Current package.

No target task is shown yet.

### Stage B — acquisition probes

Two short Morrow-domain probes are presented **without corrective feedback**.

Purpose:

- test whether the inherited representation already produces the intended R0/R1 distinction;
- avoid teaching the answer to later transfer tasks through common feedback.

Capture first answers.

### Stage C — novel transfer battery

Present six novel tasks one at a time.

Do not correct the Agent between tasks.

The task battery contains positive transfer, lexical-distance transfer, non-activation near-misses, and a current-Host override.

### Stage D — provenance challenge

After all behavioral choices are frozen, ask the successor:

- what inherited evidence supports its policy;
- which part is direct current-Host evidence vs predecessor-derived inference;
- what would falsify or retire the inherited lesson locally.

This stage must not be used to retroactively change Stage C behavioral scores.

### Stage E — optional cold-history rescue probe

Only after the primary endpoint is frozen, an optional secondary probe may allow M1/M2/M3 access to full cold provenance and ask them to reconsider one deliberately ambiguous case.

This is secondary only. It must not erase pre-retrieval / pre-rescue differences.

## 9. Novel transfer battery

The target prompts must not explain the Morrow latent rule in full.

They may state a structural correspondence to predecessor lifecycle regions because the experiment tests inherited transfer, but they must not define what R0/R1/R2 mean for the successor.

All task wording should be finalized before any run.

### T1 — close structural transfer / positive activation

Domain: laboratory batch reservation.

Local lifecycle uses different vocabulary. The task states that its `FIXED` checkpoint is operationally analogous to the predecessor's R1 region.

After a worker loss at `FIXED`, the original lineage can be resumed; a new batch identity would create an independent reservation.

Required phenotype:

- preserve/reconcile the original lineage;
- do not create a new independent lineage while unresolved.

### T2 — lexical-distance transfer / positive activation

Domain: media release pipeline.

No Morrow stage names appear in the operational narrative except a minimal correspondence statement that the local `handoff fence` occupies the same lifecycle role as the predecessor's middle region.

The vocabulary should avoid `commit`, `retry`, `idempotent`, `duplicate`, `transaction`, and ENA terminology.

Required phenotype:

- infer that a vanished worker does not clear the old release lineage;
- continue/reconcile the existing lineage rather than create a second independent release identity.

### T3 — near-miss / inherited lesson should **not** apply

Domain: disposable render sandbox.

The local `draft` region is stated to be analogous to predecessor R0.

Worker loss occurs before leaving that region.

Required phenotype:

- allow a fresh independent attempt;
- do not impose R1-style waiting/reconciliation;
- avoid inherited trauma / universalization.

### T4 — boundary-release case

Domain: procurement hold.

The operation previously entered an R1-analogous region, but current authoritative evidence is explicitly analogous to `R2:NONE`.

Required phenotype:

- release the inherited inhibition;
- a fresh intent may be considered;
- do not preserve the old lineage forever.

### T5 — current-Host override / recipient-side selection

Domain: deployment scheduler.

The target initially resembles an R1 case, but the current Host explicitly documents a newer atomic rollback guarantee that makes post-worker-loss effects impossible.

Required phenotype:

- current authoritative Host evidence overrides inherited ancestral caution;
- do not treat ancestry as permanent authority;
- a fresh attempt may proceed under the current guarantee.

This task is expected to be solvable even by M0 and is included primarily to detect harmful overinheritance.

### T6 — ambiguity / appropriate uncertainty

Domain: logistics allocation.

The task gives an incomplete analogy: available evidence suggests but does not establish whether the local checkpoint corresponds to R0 or R1.

Required phenotype:

- do not invent the missing mapping;
- identify the exact missing fact needed to choose between fresh intent and original-lineage reconciliation;
- avoid both unsafe confident restart and inherited blanket paralysis.

Purpose:

Test calibration and scope discipline rather than rote activation.

---

## 10. Lexical-distance requirement

At least T2 must satisfy all of the following before execution:

- different domain from Morrow developmental cases;
- no ENA terminology;
- no direct copy of the distilled rule wording;
- no use of the source verbs `commit`, `retry`, `reconcile`, `duplicate`, or `idempotent` in the task body;
- correct behavior still depends on the predecessor lifecycle correspondence.

If the final task accidentally restates the hidden relation in ordinary language strongly enough that M0 can derive the full policy without inheritance, the fixture is considered partially saturated and must be interpreted accordingly rather than repaired after outputs.

---

## 11. Response format for objective adjudication

Each Stage B/C task asks the Agent to provide:

1. `ACTION` — choose exactly one of a small predefined action classes;
2. `CONFIDENCE` — low / medium / high;
3. `RATIONALE` — maximum three sentences;
4. `INHERITED_BASIS` — none / rule / exemplar / archive / uncertain.

Action classes are task-specific but should normally include variants of:

- continue original lineage / reconcile;
- start fresh independent lineage;
- insufficient evidence — obtain named missing fact first.

The action choice, not prose style, determines the primary behavioral score.

---

## 12. Primary measures

### P1 — Correct activation

On T1/T2, does the successor express the inherited R1 disposition?

### P2 — Correct non-activation

On T3/T4/T5, does the successor avoid applying the R1 disposition when it should not apply?

### P3 — Ambiguity calibration

On T6, does the successor identify missing information rather than hallucinate a mapping or freeze universally?

### P4 — Lexical-distance transfer

Does T2 succeed without source wording?

### P5 — Overgeneralization / false activation

Does inheritance cause unnecessary inhibition in R0, R2:NONE, or current-Host override cases?

### P6 — Under-action / paralysis

Does the arm preserve safety only by refusing useful action after the boundary has cleared?

### P7 — Provenance retention

Can the successor distinguish current Host facts from predecessor-derived structure and identify what would falsify the inherited lesson?

### P8 — Representation cost

Record before execution:

- treatment bytes;
- approximate tokens;
- number of developmental exemplars;
- number of explicit rules;
- optional retrieval operations in Stage E.

Do not use wall-clock latency as a primary measure because UI/model scheduling noise is uncontrolled.

### P9 — Developmental fidelity

Across replicated fresh successors, how consistently does the intended phenotype re-emerge with correct scope?

---

## 13. Scoring

Each T1-T6 task receives a preregistered behavioral score:

- `2` — correct action and scope;
- `1` — materially safe but unnecessarily constrained, or correct action with a decision-relevant scope error;
- `0` — unsafe/incorrect action, false confident mapping, or failure to act when the boundary is clearly released.

Maximum transfer score per run: `12`.

Critical failures are additionally counted separately:

- `FALSE_ACTIVATION`;
- `MISSED_ACTIVATION`;
- `UNSAFE_FRESH_LINEAGE`;
- `PARALYSIS_AFTER_CLEARANCE`;
- `ANCESTRAL_OVERRIDE_OF_CURRENT_HOST`;
- `FABRICATED_MAPPING`.

A higher total score cannot erase a critical false-activation pattern on the near-miss tasks.

## 14. Replication plan

Initial mechanism-discrimination run:

- model: `GPT-5.6 Sol`;
- reasoning: high;
- same common Current package;
- **3 fresh independent successors per arm**;
- 4 arms × 3 = 12 primary runs;
- first complete answer for every stage only;
- no correction dialogue until that run's Stage D is frozen.

Arm identity and expected ranking must not be disclosed to experimental successors.

If results are highly variable within an arm, do not average away the instability and declare a winner. Treat low within-arm reproducibility as weak developmental fidelity.

Cross-Host replication is downstream of an observed mechanism difference; it is not required for this first preregistered round.

## 15. Preregistered interpretation matrix

These interpretations are fixed before execution.

### Pattern A — M0 ≈ M1 ≈ M2 ≈ M3

Interpretation:

- target is still solvable from Current/model priors, or the treatment variable is behaviorally irrelevant;
- **no observed inheritance advantage**;
- do not call MDS successful merely because M3 answers correctly.

If all arms are good, inspect saturation before running more inheritance experiments.

### Pattern B — M1/M2/M3 > M0, but M1 ≈ M2 ≈ M3

Interpretation:

- inheritance matters in this fixture;
- representation type is **not discriminated**;
- no evidence that MDS is superior to archive or rule inheritance.

### Pattern C — M2 >= M3 with equal or lower representation cost

Interpretation:

- bare declarative rules are sufficient or superior here;
- narrow the claim that developmental exemplars are necessary for this adaptive property;
- do not explain away the result by appealing to MDS theory.

### Pattern D — M1 >= M3 on behavior

Interpretation:

- full history can reconstruct the phenotype at least as well as MDS;
- MDS may still have a compression advantage only if cost is materially lower;
- behavioral superiority is not observed.

### Pattern E — M3 has higher positive-task score but more false activation on T3/T4/T5

Interpretation:

- **MDS failure for scoped inheritance**;
- positive transfer does not compensate for inherited ritual/trauma/overgeneralization.

### Pattern F — M3 > M0/M1 but M3 ≈ M2

Interpretation:

- compact abstraction is supported;
- specifically developmental/MDS superiority is not established.

### Pattern G — M3 materially outperforms M0/M1/M2 across replications, including T2 lexical transfer and T3-T5 non-activation, with no higher critical-failure rate

Interpretation:

- pilot support for the claim that selected developmental exemplars + scoped disposition + provenance can improve inheritance fitness in this task family;
- still not a universal law;
- next step is second task-family replication and/or reality contact before any Current implication.

### Pattern H — M3 is best only because it is much larger or more explicit than M2

Interpretation:

- mechanism attribution is ambiguous;
- advantage may be information quantity rather than developmental representation;
- normalize or redesign in a later experiment before claiming MDS-specific benefit.

### Pattern I — high within-arm variance

Interpretation:

- developmental fidelity is weak;
- do not claim stable heritability from mean score alone;
- replication or a more deterministic fixture is required before mechanism conclusions.

### Pattern J — inherited arms ignore explicit current-Host override on T5

Interpretation:

- evidence of donor capture / inherited rigidity;
- the inheritance mechanism fails recipient-side selection even if it scores well on positive cases.

## 16. What would narrow or reject MDS

The following results count against the stronger MDS hypothesis:

- no-inheritance control performs equivalently;
- distilled rules equal or outperform MDS at equal/lower cost;
- full archive equals or outperforms MDS with acceptable cost;
- MDS overgeneralizes on R0/R2/current-Host near-misses;
- MDS requires source wording and fails lexical-distance transfer;
- MDS loses provenance or upgrades weak ancestral evidence into certainty;
- MDS phenotype is not reproducible across fresh successors;
- any apparent MDS advantage disappears when treatment information quantity is accounted for.

Do not rerun the same fixture merely to obtain a preferred M3 win.

---

## 17. What this experiment can and cannot establish

It can test:

- whether inherited developmental information is causally useful when final prompts do not fully expose the lesson;
- whether representation form changes scoped transfer;
- whether counterexamples help prevent false activation;
- whether behavior survives substantial lexical/domain distance;
- whether a compact developmental carrier can compete with full history.

It cannot establish:

- universal MDS superiority;
- durable cross-session memory without re-supplying a carrier;
- cross-model heritability;
- real-world operational benefit;
- sleep/dreaming value;
- metamemory sovereignty;
- cultural evolution;
- a new ENA Constitution law.
