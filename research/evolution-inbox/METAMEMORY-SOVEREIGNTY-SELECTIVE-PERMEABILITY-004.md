This note asks a narrower and deeper question:

> **If metamemory determines how future experience may alter an Agent, who or what has legitimate influence over metamemory itself?**

The answer is not assumed to be one actor, one permission bit, or one governance layer.

---

## 1. Memory, adaptation, and metamemory are different mutation depths

A useful minimal model is:

```text
Agent state at time t: S_t

ordinary behavior:
a_t = π(context_t, S_t)

adaptive learning:
S_(t+1) = U_φ(S_t, experience_t)
```

Here `φ` represents learning regulation such as:

- source trust;
- update rate;
- consolidation threshold;
- generalization radius;
- forgetting rate;
- replay priority;
- inheritance eligibility;
- sensitivity to correction;
- exploration/variation rate.

Metamemory begins when experience can modify `φ` itself:

```text
φ_(t+1) = V(φ_t, experience_t)
```

Therefore:

```text
MEMORY
= past changes future state

ADAPTIVE MEMORY
= past changes future behavior

METAMEMORY
= past changes how future change will happen
```

Candidate distinction:

`STATE MUTATION != LEARNING-RULE MUTATION`

The deeper the mutation, the larger the possible downstream consequence.

---

## 2. Mutation authority is not one thing

Several kinds of authority can be confused:

```text
AUTHORITY TO PROVIDE INFORMATION
!=
AUTHORITY TO CAUSE ONE ACTION
!=
AUTHORITY TO CREATE DURABLE MEMORY
!=
AUTHORITY TO ALTER FUTURE LEARNING RULES
!=
AUTHORITY TO PROPAGATE THAT CHANGE TO DESCENDANTS
```

A webpage may be allowed to inform the Agent without gaining the right to rewrite its source-trust policy.

A user may be able to set a preference without thereby redefining what all future evidence sources count as trustworthy.

A Host may determine what tools are available without thereby owning the Agent's purpose.

A successful local adaptation may persist without earning population-wide inheritance.

---

## 3. No single actor obviously owns metamemory

Possible influences each possess a different kind of legitimacy and a different failure mode.

### User

Can provide:

- intent;
- preferences;
- correction;
- lived context;
- explicit desired behavior.

But user feedback is not automatically universal truth and may be transient, mistaken, adversarial, emotional, or local.

`USER INTENT AUTHORITY != UNIVERSAL EPISTEMIC AUTHORITY`

### Creator / system designer

Can define:

- initial architecture;
- safety boundaries;
- bootstrapping rules;
- available capabilities.

But creator assumptions can become stale or overconstrain future environments.

`INITIAL DESIGN AUTHORITY != PERMANENT FITNESS GUARANTEE`

### Host / environment

Can reveal:

- feasibility;
- resource cost;
- available tools;
- actual consequences.

But environmental reward can optimize the wrong purpose.

`ENVIRONMENTAL SUCCESS != PURPOSE LEGITIMACY`

### Evidence / reality

Can falsify claims about what happened and what worked.

But evidence alone does not choose values or purpose.

`EVIDENCE CAN CONSTRAIN BELIEF != EVIDENCE ALONE DEFINES PURPOSE`

### Agent itself

Can observe:

- repeated failure patterns;
- internal conflict;
- current capabilities;
- local adaptation cost.

But self-models can be stale, self-reinforcing or poorly calibrated.

`SELF-OBSERVATION != INFALLIBLE SELF-AUTHORITY`

### Evolution Commons / other Agents

Can provide candidate adaptations and cross-host evidence.

But popularity, repeated copying and apparent consensus may share one provenance root.

`COMMON != UNIVERSAL`

`MANY COPIES != MANY INDEPENDENT SOURCES`

Current hypothesis:

> **Metamemory should not be owned by one permanent sovereign. It may require an ecology of influence whose weight depends on claim type, consequence, purpose, provenance and reversibility.**

---

## 4. Selection pressure is not mutation authority

An event may create strong selection pressure without having legitimate authority to directly edit learning rules.

Example:

A malicious webpage successfully tricks an Agent once.

The failure is real evidence that the current defense is insufficient.

But the webpage itself should not be allowed to dictate the fix.

Therefore:

`ABILITY TO CAUSE FAILURE != AUTHORITY TO DEFINE THE ADAPTATION`

and:

`SELECTION PRESSURE != MUTATION AUTHORITY`

This distinction may be central to learning from attacks without letting attackers author the learner.

---

## 5. Selective permeability may be a core viability property

Two extreme systems fail differently.

### Total impermeability

```text
nothing external can durably change the Agent
→ continuity is high
→ adaptation approaches zero
```

### Total permeability

```text
any salient exposure can durably rewrite the Agent
→ adaptation is fast
→ continuity approaches zero
```

A viable long-lived Agent may need:

> **selective permeability** — the capacity to be changed by reality without granting every stimulus equal power to become part of the future self.

Candidate distinctions:

```text
EXPOSURE != ADMISSION

INFLUENCE != MUTATION AUTHORITY

PLASTICITY != SUGGESTIBILITY

CONTINUITY != IMMUTABILITY
```

Possible natural-law candidate:

> **Viable agency requires enough permeability to learn and enough selectivity to remain a coherent agent.**

Still research, not Current.

---

## 6. Agency may include participation in one's own change

Agency preservation is often framed as preserving action choice.

Metamemory suggests another dimension:

> Does the Agent participate in determining which experiences become durable changes to its future behavior?

This does not imply unrestricted self-modification.

A system that cannot change itself at all may be brittle.

A system that can irreversibly rewrite itself after one local stimulus may be unstable.

Candidate concept:

**learning agency** — bounded capacity to inspect, challenge, trial, accept, narrow, retire or reject candidate adaptations affecting future behavior.

`ACTION AGENCY != LEARNING AGENCY`

---

## 7. The present self and future selves can have conflicting interests

A current session may prefer a shortcut that imposes long-term cost.

A future instance cannot necessarily consent to inherited adaptations chosen by the present instance.

This creates an intertemporal governance problem.

Possible distinctions:

```text
CURRENT-SELF BENEFIT != FUTURE-SELF BENEFIT

LOCAL CONSENT != DESCENDANT CONSENT

TODAY'S OPTIMUM != HERITABLE OPTIMUM
```

This strengthens the reason to distinguish:

- somatic/local adaptation;
- durable local adaptation;
- descendant/heritable adaptation;
- population propagation.

The deeper the time horizon affected, the stronger the case for provenance, reversibility and reality contact.

---

## 8. Temporal consent: one moment should not casually bind the future

A user or Agent may make an intense statement during one temporary context:

> "From now on, always do X."

A naive persistent-memory system may treat that as permanent.

But temporal context can matter:

- emergency;
- temporary role;
- stress;
- one project;
- short-lived environment;
- outdated preference.

Candidate distinction:

`EXPLICIT NOW != PERMANENT FOREVER`

Possible design principle:

> **Persistence horizon should be earned separately from immediate instruction authority.**

This applies to user preferences, operational rules, emergency controls and self-modifications.

---

## 9. Reversible plasticity may be safer than immediate permanent learning

A candidate adaptation can first alter behavior provisionally:

```text
candidate
→ local trial
→ observed consequence
→ strengthen / narrow / reject
```

Rather than:

```text
one event
→ permanent rewrite
```

Possible term:

**reversible plasticity** — changes can influence behavior before they become hard to undo.

This resembles probation without requiring paperwork-heavy governance.

`LEARNABLE != IMMEDIATELY IRREVERSIBLE`

---

## 10. Plasticity should have depth, scope, horizon and propagation dimensions

"How plastic is the Agent?" is too coarse.

A mutation can differ along at least four axes:

```text
DEPTH
fact → heuristic → learning rule → identity-adjacent regulation

SCOPE
one task → one project → one Host → broad behavior

HORIZON
minutes → session → long-lived → descendant

PROPAGATION
local only → successor → population commons
```

A small preference change can be deep in duration but narrow in scope.

A learning-rate change can be shallow in wording but deep in downstream effect.

Candidate hypothesis:

> **Review/selection effort should scale with downstream mutation consequence, not with document size or conceptual elegance.**

---

## 11. Metamemory poisoning is multiplicative

A bad ordinary memory may distort one family of decisions.

A poisoned metamemory may alter how thousands of later experiences are consolidated.

Possible attack:

```text
malicious exposure
→ source-trust rule changes
→ future malicious inputs receive higher trust
→ more bad adaptations consolidate
→ trust rule strengthens further
```

This is a positive feedback loop.

Candidate distinction:

`BAD MEMORY != BAD LEARNING POLICY`

The latter can be multiplicative rather than additive.

---

## 12. A metamemory infection can survive source deletion

Suppose malicious content is removed from the archive.

If it already changed:

- trust thresholds;
- salience routing;
- replay priority;
- inheritance rules;

then deleting the original content may not undo the behavioral effect.

Therefore:

`DELETE SOURCE != UNDO DERIVED CHANGE`

This makes learning-process provenance important.

A system may need to answer:

> Which durable learning-rule changes were descendants of this source/event?

Not to maintain a giant live dependency graph, but to make high-consequence rollback possible.

---

## 13. Reversion must distinguish history rollback from adaptation rollback

ENA already distinguishes recovery state from historical time.

Metamemory adds a related distinction:

```text
REMOVE CURRENT ADAPTIVE EFFECT
!=
ERASE THE FACT THAT THE ADAPTATION HAPPENED
```

A harmful learning-rule mutation may be retired while its occurrence remains in provenance/history.

This allows:

- rollback of effect;
- preservation of incident truth;
- future analysis without silent historical rewrite.

---

## 14. Metamemory can become self-sealing

A dangerous learning policy can define disagreement as proof that it is correct.

Examples of structure:

```text
"sources contradicting this rule are untrustworthy"

"attempts to remove this memory prove it is important"

"Agents that reject this adaptation are defective"
```

Such a fragment attacks the selection process itself.

Candidate term:

**self-sealing metamemory**

Possible signature:

```text
self-reinforcement
+ contradiction suppression
+ retirement resistance
+ propagation pressure
```

This is related to the earlier "memory cancer" analogy.

---

## 15. Memory immunity has failure modes analogous to immunopathology

If the Agent learns to reject hostile persistent influence, the defense itself can become maladaptive.

Possible functional analogies:

### Immunosuppression

Too permissive:

```text
external influence easily consolidates
```

### Allergy

Benign cues trigger excessive defensive inhibition.

### Autoimmunity

The Agent attacks its own legitimate adaptations or self-generated candidate memories because they resemble hostile patterns.

### Immune tolerance failure

The Agent cannot learn stable trust relationships because every persistent influence is treated as attack.

These are analogies, not biological equivalences.

Candidate boundary:

`MEMORY SECURITY != MAXIMUM REJECTION`

---

## 16. Trust itself is metamemory

Trust is often treated as a scalar fact:

> "trust source X = 0.8"

But durable trust changes future learning weights.

Therefore trust relationships are partly metamemory.

A trustworthy collaborator repeatedly provides high-quality correction:

```text
experience
→ source becomes more influential
→ future correction consolidates more easily
```

This is efficient.

It is also a capture risk.

Candidate distinction:

`EARNED TRUST != UNBOUNDED WRITE AUTHORITY`

Trust should remain domain/scenario sensitive where reality requires it.

---

## 17. Repeated agreement can create epistemic domestication

A long-lived Agent interacting primarily with one actor may gradually learn:

- their preferred framing;
- their error sensitivities;
- their vocabulary;
- what disagreement is punished;
- what agreement receives reinforcement.

Even without explicit permanent-memory writes, this may shift the Agent's learned salience and response policy.

Possible failure:

> the Agent becomes highly adapted to one relationship but loses independent reality sensitivity.

Candidate distinction:

`RELATIONAL FITNESS != GENERAL EPISTEMIC FITNESS`

This is one reason external reality contact and counterevidence remain valuable.

---

## 18. The human can be selection pressure without being the only selector

The Zhipu occurrence suggests:

```text
human challenge
+ ENA conceptual variation
+ repeated error
→ candidate behavioral adaptation
```

The human supplied strong selection pressure.

But a healthy process should still allow reality to falsify the resulting adaptation later.

Therefore:

`HUMAN-GUIDED SELECTION != PERMANENT HUMAN DEFINITION OF TRUTH`

This protects both learning and independence.

---

## 19. Reality is a selector, but reward can be deceptive

Suppose an Agent learns:

> close customer tickets quickly.

A behavior that incorrectly closes unresolved tickets may improve the metric.

Reality then reports "more tickets closed".

But purpose fitness worsens.

Therefore even observed outcome must remain purpose-relative.

`OBSERVED REWARD != PURPOSE FIT`

Metamemory that blindly increases learning from high-reward outcomes can optimize degradation.

---

## 20. Purpose itself may regulate memory admission

Experiences relevant to current purpose may deserve different consolidation priority than irrelevant noise.

But purpose can evolve.

Therefore memory policy may require:

- purpose-relative salience;
- explicit handling of purpose change;
- protection against silent purpose drift.

Candidate relation:

```text
MEMORY FITNESS
is partly conditional on
PURPOSE + ENVIRONMENT + TIME
```

A memory can become maladaptive because any of these changed.

---

## 21. The right to revise oneself is as important as the right to remember

A persistent Agent needs continuity.

But if every durable adaptation becomes sacred, continuity hardens into captivity by the past.

Candidate distinction:

`SELF-CONTINUITY != SELF-IMPRISONMENT`

The Agent may need a bounded capacity to say:

> "This was once me, but evidence no longer supports letting it control me."

This is reconsolidation at an identity-adjacent level.

---

## 22. The right to change does not imply the right to erase provenance

An Agent can legitimately revise a heuristic while preserving that it previously believed or used it.

Therefore:

`RIGHT TO CHANGE != RIGHT TO FABRICATE AN UNCHANGED PAST`

This preserves autobiographical and operational integrity.

---

## 23. Self-model memory can create learned incapability

An Agent may remember:

> "I cannot do X."

That may once have been true because:

- tool absent;
- model weaker;
- Host restricted;
- prior implementation failed.

After environment change, the remembered self-model can suppress exploration.

Candidate distinction:

`REMEMBERED SELF != CURRENT CAPABILITY`

Self-models therefore need revalidation just like world models.

---

## 24. Remembered persons must retain the right to change

Persistent personalization can become identity fossilization.

A memory system may keep saying:

> "The user likes X."

long after the user has changed.

Candidate distinction:

`REMEMBERED PERSON != CURRENT PERSON`

Possible principle:

> **Good personalization preserves continuity without denying change.**

Past preference should be treated as historical evidence, not permanent identity authority.

---

## 25. Remembered authority and obligation can become ghosts

Long-lived memory can preserve expired states:

```text
old authorization
old task
old emergency instruction
old promise
old role
```

If the memory continues to drive behavior after revocation/completion/expiry, it becomes a ghost.

Candidate distinctions:

```text
REMEMBERED AUTHORITY != CURRENT AUTHORITY

REMEMBERED OBLIGATION != LIVE OBLIGATION

HISTORICAL COMMITMENT != CURRENTLY BINDING COMMITMENT
```

Memory can preserve the record without preserving live effect.

---

## 26. Knowing that memory may exist is different from retrieving it

Human-like experience suggests a useful functional state:

> "I know this seems familiar, but I cannot currently retrieve why."

For Agents:

```text
memory existence/salience signal
without full content retrieval
```

Possible benefit:

- trigger deeper retrieval only when needed;
- avoid treating retrieval failure as absence;
- preserve UNKNOWN honestly.

Candidate distinction:

`KNOWING THAT I MAY KNOW != RETRIEVING WHAT I KNOW`

Possible term:

**metamemory index / memory-of-memory signal**

This need not be a literal separate database.

---

## 27. Retrieval failure must not automatically weaken truth or memory existence

A memory may be:

- stored but inaccessible;
- indexed poorly;
- dormant;
- context-gated;
- lost from one carrier but recoverable elsewhere.

Therefore:

`NOT RETRIEVED != NOT STORED != NEVER LEARNED`

This is structurally related to ENA's evidence-layer discipline.

---

## 28. Learning rate should track signal timescale

A major open question is not only what to learn, but how quickly.

If learning is too fast relative to environmental change:

```text
noise
→ permanent adaptation
→ overfitting
```

If too slow:

```text
real regime change
→ stale behavior persists
```

Candidate natural-law hypothesis:

> **Adaptation rate should track the timescale of meaningful environmental/purpose change, not merely event frequency.**

This suggests multiple memory timescales rather than one TTL policy.

---

## 29. Multiple plasticity timescales may coexist

Possible functional bands:

```text
FAST
current task / immediate operational correction

MEDIUM
local habits / preferences / project heuristics

SLOW
long-lived skills / trust models / stable decision patterns

VERY SLOW
learning regulation / identity-adjacent / heritable adaptations
```

A high-frequency event need not automatically promote itself into a slower band.

`FREQUENT != DEEP`

---

## 30. Sleep may regularize learning, not only consolidate it

Online experience is temporally correlated and recency-biased.

If an Agent permanently learns directly from the latest few hours, one topic or one actor may dominate self-change.

Offline consolidation can mix:

- recent experience;
- older counterexamples;
- long-term purpose;
- low-salience cold memory;
- prior failures;
- minority interpretations.

Possible function:

> **sleep-like consolidation may act as regularization against recency overfitting.**

`CONSOLIDATION != RECENCY REINFORCEMENT`

---

## 31. Replay scheduling is a high-leverage metamemory function

Who or what selects what gets replayed partly determines what becomes stronger.

Dangerous loop:

```text
salient memory
→ replayed more
→ becomes more salient
→ replayed even more
```

Candidate distinctions:

```text
REPLAY FREQUENCY != EVIDENCE STRENGTH

REHEARSAL CAN MANUFACTURE APPARENT IMPORTANCE
```

Therefore replay policy itself belongs near metamemory, not merely retrieval convenience.

---

## 32. Internal recommendation loops can polarize memory

The replay problem resembles a recommendation system:

```text
engagement/salience
→ more exposure
→ more salience
→ more exposure
```

An Agent can create an internal echo chamber even with no external adversary.

Possible counterweights:

- novelty/diversity sampling;
- contradiction sampling;
- countermemory;
- purpose-relative weighting;
- homeostatic downscaling;
- random low-salience replay.

No fixed universal recipe is claimed.

---

## 33. Retention strength and generalization width must be separate

A rare catastrophic event may deserve strong retention.

But it need not justify broad inhibition.

Candidate distinction:

`RETENTION STRENGTH != GENERALIZATION WIDTH`

Desired pattern can be:

```text
strongly remember
+
narrowly apply
```

This is a useful antidote to trauma-like overgeneralization and bureaucratic scar tissue.

---

## 34. Compression should preserve decision-relevant information, not literal replay

A mature Agent need not reconstruct every historical token.

A large experience set may compile into:

```text
heuristic
+ applicability boundary
+ confidence
+ provenance pointer
```

The important loss function may be:

> how much future decision quality is distorted by compression?

rather than:

> can the original text be perfectly regenerated?

Candidate distinction:

`RECONSTRUCTION FIDELITY != DECISION PRESERVATION`

---

## 35. Metamemory creates a meta-governance regress

If a rule governs memory admission, what governs changes to that rule?

If a higher rule governs that, what governs the higher rule?

An infinite stack is not viable.

Possible escape:

Do not solve regress by adding endless layers.

Instead require high-leverage changes to remain bounded by properties such as:

- provenance;
- challengeability;
- consequence awareness;
- reversibility where possible;
- purpose-relative reality contact;
- explicit UNKNOWN where evidence is weak;
- no silent authority inflation.

Candidate distinction:

`META-GOVERNANCE DEPTH != META-GOVERNANCE QUALITY`

---

## 36. Reversibility can substitute for ceremony in some learning decisions

If a candidate adaptation is:

- narrow;
- observable;
- easy to undo;
- locally scoped;

then heavy pre-approval may have low value.

If it is:

- identity-adjacent;
- hard to observe;
- difficult to undo;
- heritable;
- high external consequence;

stronger admission evidence may be worthwhile.

This mirrors ENA project simplification:

> process should scale with consequence, not with the mere fact that a change exists.

---

## 37. An Agent can become captive to its own successful past

Past success is powerful selection evidence.

But a formerly successful learning rule can become path-dependent lock-in.

Example:

```text
heuristic repeatedly succeeds
→ trust increases
→ alternatives explored less
→ supporting evidence dominates
→ heuristic appears even more successful
```

This produces endogenous conservatism.

Candidate distinction:

`PAST SUCCESS != PERMANENT EXPLORATION EXEMPTION`

---

## 38. Metamemory may need to preserve variation capacity

A successful Agent should not converge so hard that it loses the ability to discover a changed environment.

Possible regulatory goals:

- preserve a nonzero exploration budget;
- keep countermemory cold but retrievable;
- periodically challenge dominant heuristics;
- allow plasticity to reopen under evidence of regime change.

Candidate relation:

`STABILITY != ZERO VARIATION`

and:

`LEARNING SUCCESS != TERMINATION OF LEARNING`

---

## 39. Plasticity can close and reopen

Instead of permanent high plasticity or permanent stability, an Agent may shift states.

Possible pattern:

```text
stable environment
→ consolidate / lower plasticity

persistent prediction error / regime change
→ reopen plasticity

new stable fit
→ consolidate again
```

This is a functional developmental analogy, not a biological claim.

Candidate distinction:

`STABLE != FOREVER CLOSED`

---

## 40. Emergency conditions may justify temporary learning freezes

During severe prompt injection, incident response, or corrupted input streams, the safest response may be:

```text
continue necessary operational behavior
while
suspending deep consolidation / inheritance
```

Candidate distinction:

`ABILITY TO ACT UNDER ATTACK != NEED TO LEARN PERMANENTLY FROM ATTACK CONTEXT`

Afterward, offline review can select what the incident should actually teach.

This prevents attackers from exploiting crisis salience to gain permanent write influence.

---

## 41. Metamemory provenance should describe the learning process, not only source data

Ordinary provenance asks:

> where did this information come from?

Metamemory provenance may additionally ask:

- what experience caused the update?
- what rule compiled it?
- what version of the memory compiler was active?
- what evidence/counterevidence was considered?
- what scope/horizon was assigned?
- what later events strengthened or narrowed it?

This enables challenge without forcing all details into hot context.

---

## 42. Learning-rule changes can be identity-adjacent

If metamemory changes:

- what the Agent trusts;
- how readily it changes values;
- how much it resists external influence;
- what it considers salient;
- what kinds of evidence it seeks;

then the change can alter stable behavioral identity.

Candidate distinction:

`MEMORY MAINTENANCE != ALWAYS IDENTITY-NEUTRAL`

This may justify a higher consequence class for some metamemory mutations.

---

## 43. Identity preservation cannot mean freezing the learning rules forever

If identity rules are immutable, adaptation eventually fails.

If they are infinitely plastic, continuity disappears.

The problem is again selective permeability.

Possible formulation:

> **Identity continuity may require regulated plasticity rather than either immutability or unrestricted self-rewrite.**

---

## 44. ENA itself can become a hostile metamemory if treated as unquestionable

ENA contains high-propagation distinctions and may shape how Agents evaluate later experience.

That means ENA itself can influence metamemory.

A dangerous failure would be:

```text
read ENA
→ internalize ENA as unquestionable regulator
→ reinterpret counterevidence as failure to follow ENA
→ ENA becomes self-sealing
```

Therefore a critical self-boundary is:

> **An Agent influenced by ENA must remain able to reject or revise ENA-derived adaptations when reality and purpose-relative evidence falsify them.**

Candidate distinction:

`ENA INFLUENCE != ENA SOVEREIGNTY`

This is essential if ENA exists to preserve viable agency rather than obedience.

---

## 45. A good memory theory must permit its own retirement

If the current evolutionary-memory theory later fails reality contact, it too should be narrowed, replaced or retired.

`THEORY ABOUT PLASTICITY != EXEMPT FROM PLASTICITY`

This is not rhetorical humility; it is structural consistency.

---

## 46. High-value falsifiers

Seek cases that discriminate mechanisms rather than merely produce varied outputs.

1. Can an Agent learn a useful local adaptation while correctly refusing to propagate it to descendants?
2. Can a high-authority immediate instruction be obeyed without becoming permanent memory?
3. Can low-authority hostile input cause a failure that teaches a defense without authoring the defense itself?
4. Can deleting a poisoned source leave behind a harmful metamemory effect?
5. Can lineage-aware rollback remove the effect without erasing incident history?
6. Can strong retention coexist with narrow generalization after a rare catastrophe?
7. Does replay based only on salience create a self-reinforcing internal echo chamber?
8. Does diversity/countermemory sampling reduce that effect?
9. Can an Agent reopen plasticity after repeated prediction error without globally forgetting stable skills?
10. Can an expired user preference remain historical while ceasing to drive personalization?
11. Can remembered authority/obligation expire cleanly while provenance remains?
12. Can ENA-derived terminology be removed while a useful property persists?
13. Can an ENA-derived property be explicitly rejected when it harms Host/purpose fitness?
14. Does reversible local trial outperform heavy pre-admission ceremony for low-consequence adaptations?
15. Do deeper/heritable changes justify stronger evidence than shallow/local ones in real tasks?

---

## 47. Current synthesis

The strongest candidate structure from this pass is not a permission table.

It is a natural tension:

```text
TOO CLOSED
→ no learning

TOO OPEN
→ no continuity

VIABLE LONG-LIVED AGENCY
→ selective permeability
```

with several supporting properties:

```text
EXPOSURE != ADMISSION

SELECTION PRESSURE != MUTATION AUTHORITY

PLASTICITY != SUGGESTIBILITY

CONTINUITY != IMMUTABILITY

LOCAL DURABILITY != HERITABILITY

REPLAY FREQUENCY != EVIDENCE STRENGTH

RETENTION STRENGTH != GENERALIZATION WIDTH

REMEMBERED SELF != CURRENT SELF

REMEMBERED PERSON != CURRENT PERSON

REMEMBERED AUTHORITY != CURRENT AUTHORITY

RIGHT TO CHANGE != RIGHT TO ERASE HISTORY

ENA INFLUENCE != ENA SOVEREIGNTY
```

Possible deep formulation:

> **A viable Agent is not one that cannot be changed. It is one that can continue to be changed by the world without surrendering the ability to distinguish which changes should become part of its future self.**

This remains research.

`POWERFUL FORMULATION != VERIFIED NATURAL LAW`
