# Lessons and Reminders

## Product boundary
Maintainer instructions are not product content by default. Internalize the instruction in the quality of the product; do not print the teacher's directions on the exam paper.

## Legacy structure
Do not infer product necessity from historical depth or entanglement. A concept can be deeply embedded because the project grew around it, not because Agents intrinsically need it.

## Derivation order
Derive from real Agent problems first. Use legacy material afterward to catch meaningful omissions and regressions.

## Simplicity
The desired product should actually be concise, understandable, useful, and grounded in real work. Do not solve that requirement by adding a section that tells adopters the product should be concise, understandable, useful, and grounded.

## Vocabulary
Do not create or inherit specialist ENA vocabulary unless the underlying concept independently earns the cost. Prefer direct behavior over named abstractions.

## Practical HOW
If a practical HOW is necessary to use ENA, it belongs naturally in the ENA product. Do not recreate a second product repository merely to hold HOW.

## Repository roles
- `guytogay/ENA`: new product home.
- `guytogay/evolution-native-agent-architecture`: legacy Current + history/research/evidence.
- `guytogay/human-ai-workbench`: general human-AI project-working method.
- `guytogay/ena-field-guide`: sunset; preserve useful evidence, then archive.

## Cleanup
Archive/delete/transfer according to actual role. Git history is allowed to carry history; live product surfaces do not need to preserve obsolete project machinery for sentimental continuity.

## Succession
Do not allow a future handoff to reduce this correction to a style preference. It is a product-boundary and derivation-order correction.
