# Recent Three Rounds

## Round 1 — Capability coverage versus representation

The owner corrected the interpretation that some legacy practical families might simply be skipped. Required capability/protection should not be lost merely to keep v0.4 short. The real freedom is in representation: a capability may be covered directly, by a card, by machinery/external control, by cold HOW, or by a combination. Separate files/cards are not mandatory.

## Round 2 — Vocabulary and product style

The owner observed that ENA has invented too many unusual terms and wants the new ENA to be simple, clear, practical, and grounded in ordinary work. The session initially responded by writing explicit simplicity/plain-language rules into ENA rebuild content.

The owner then corrected this as a category error: instructions about how the maintainer should write/design ENA are not automatically ENA product content. If the product is actually clear, it does not need to teach the user that its authors were told to write clearly.

## Round 3 — Embedded contamination and clean-root consequence

The owner identified a deeper issue: project/session-maintenance material has not merely accumulated beside ENA; some of it may have become structurally embedded in ENA itself, like a coin inserted into a tree trunk and later wrapped by layers of growth.

Examples include handoff/takeover concerns, project continuity, maintainer workflow, research orchestration, and owner instructions to prior sessions. Some resulting concepts may now look native because later ENA grew around them.

The owner directed actual cleanup: archive what should be archived, delete what should be deleted, transfer what belongs elsewhere. If separation inside the existing repository is too costly or unreliable, the owner is willing to create a new repository for a clean v0.4 product.

Consequent design change: legacy ENA is evidence/regression material, not the v0.4 blueprint. Pause mechanical Action Card derivation from old families and re-derive from ENA purpose + real Agent problems first.
